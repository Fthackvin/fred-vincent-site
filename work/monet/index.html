Monet

Creating a desirable consumer brand for neuro tech people would actually want to wear.

  • Brand strategy
  • Creative direction
  • Identity
  • Naming
Fred 0:52

Peter and the team are developing a wearable that could passively read brain activity but also addictively improve peoples focus and creativity. They needed a launch brand.

The challenge was to make something unfamiliar and deeply personal feel desirable, trustworthy and part of everyday life, without falling into the clinical, wellness or science-fiction language surrounding every other similar product neuro-tech.

After auditing the category alongside references from fashion, culture, wellness and luxury, we workshopped what mattered most to the company, what customers should feel on first encounter and how that relationship might change over time which gave some clear tensions:

  • Advanced, but not sci-fi.
  • Premium, but not stuffy.
  • Human, but not generic wellness.
  • Performance, without sports culture.
  • Visible enough to be desirable yet quiet enough to fade into the background when needed.

Neuro-tech to fashion

I deliberately moved away from brains, neural imagery and the familiar visual language of neurotech.

Instead, the identity began to borrow from fashion, art and culture: expressive marks paired with restrained typography, generous space, rich greens and neutrals, and small flashes of brighter colour.

The imagery focused less on what the technology was doing and more on what it might enable: concentration, movement, creativity and flow.

Building a world around the product

The identity was tested beyond a logo from the beginning.

On the street it could be rougher and more mysterious. Around the product it became quieter and more precise whilst in the physical spaces it became warmer, tactile and social.

That led to ideas for apparel, campaign imagery, packaging and digital experiences, but also a different kind of retail environment: part store, part listening bar, part creative space.

Outcome

The work gave the team an MVP identity they could immediately start applying across fundraising, product, website and go-to-market material.
