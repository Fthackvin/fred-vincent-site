Warburg's Atlas

Work in progress

A tool for reading images deeply and linking them, modelled on Aby Warburg's Mnemosyne Atlas, that I can share with clients and keep here on the site.

The idea

Warburg spent the last years of his life, up to 1929, pinning photographs to black cloth panels, a Renaissance fresco next to a Roman coin next to a newspaper advert, so that you could see a gesture or a figure travel across centuries. He never finished it, and the panels survive only as photographs, but the way of looking has stayed with me.

A lot of brand and product work starts the same way, gathering references and putting them side by side until something connects, and most of the tools for it are either a mood board or a graph. I want something in between, where the reason two images belong together is written down and kept.

A plate, not a canvas

It's a plate rather than a node or canvas tool. The weight of a thread shows how strong the link between two images is, and making a connection means adding an image and saying why it resonates with the one beside it.

The whole library sits in rows along the bottom, a bit like Guess Who, so you can pull one out at random and the library nudges a few relevant or similar ones towards you, including images from open collections and Are.na.

Where it is now

The design of the plate is underway, and the bigger piece of work is behind it, the connections to image collections and how the reading of an image is done carefully and with cultural sensitivity. I'll add to this page as it comes together.
