Week to week

Nov 2026

In Kaizen, a training plan arrives as one week with one number, and the rest of the block is a timeline you flick through, a week at a time.

A week, not a plan

Most training plans hand you the whole block at once, sixteen weeks of sessions in a grid, and it's a lot to hold in your head when all you really want to know is what to do this week. Kaizen generates the suggested runs, then shows you this week's target, one distance to aim for, with the days laid out underneath it.

The ring at the top fills as you run and the number counts down, so the week has a shape you can see at a glance, and the plan stays fully flexible, so you can change it to fit your day.

A timeline you flick

The weeks sit side by side along the top, so moving through the plan is a flick from one week to the next rather than a menu or a calendar. You can look ahead to see the target grow as the race gets closer, or look back at what you did, and you're always one flick from this week again.
